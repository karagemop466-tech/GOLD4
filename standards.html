U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC) · FTC Consumer Guidance

FTC Consumer Advice — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry ↗

  • Defines 24-karat purity scale (14K = 14 parts gold, 10 parts alloy) and requires trademark stamp beside any quality mark.
  • Distinguishes solid karat gold from hollow gold and from plated, gold-filled, rolled gold plate, gold overlay, and vermeil (gold over sterling silver).
  • Advises buyers to get karat weight, gram weight, and return policy in writing before purchasing.

Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (NARA / FTC) · 16 CFR §§ 23.1–23.18 (incl. § 23.3 Gold, § 23.4 Vermeil, § 23.7 Rhodium, § 23.8 Multi-Metal, § 23.9 Quality Marks)

16 CFR Part 23 — Guides for the Jewelry, Precious Metals, and Pewter Industries ↗

  • 16 CFR § 23.3: unqualified 'Gold' means fine (24K) gold through and through; a gold alloy must carry its karat designation immediately before the word 'Gold' and with equal conspicuousness; hollow-centred articles should not be marked 'solid'. There is NO 10-karat floor for calling a gold alloy 'karat gold' — § 23.3(c)(1) itself gives '9 Karat Gold' as acceptable — 10 karat survives only as the minimum fineness for plating, gold-filled and vermeil (§ 23.3(c)(2)-(3), § 23.4(b)).
  • 16 CFR § 23.4: Vermeil requires sterling silver (>=92.5% Ag) coated with >=2.5 microns of >=10K gold — never solid gold.
  • 16 CFR § 23.7: it is unfair or deceptive to fail to disclose a surface-layer application of rhodium on a product marked or described as precious metal — the relevant rule for rhodium-plated white gold. 16 CFR § 23.8: where a product contains more than one precious metal, the relative quantity of each must not be misrepresented, and metals should be listed from greatest to least by weight (e.g., an 18K shank with platinum prongs).
  • 15 U.S.C. § 297(b) (National Stamping Act): a quality mark indicating gold or silver must be accompanied by the maker's registered trademark (or the maker's name), applied by the same means, in lettering at least as large, and as close as possible to the quality mark. § 297(b)(B) adds that where a multi-part article has parts of different quality, each other part must carry a matching quality mark. The FTC's consumer page states the practical version: don't buy a gold piece if the trademark is missing beside the karat mark.

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) · 15 U.S.C. § 1666 / Regulation Z (12 CFR § 1026.13)

CFPB Official Guidance — Disputing Credit Card Charges (Fair Credit Billing Act) ↗

  • Pay exclusively by credit card (never wire, Zelle, crypto, or debit): preserves statutory billing-error and goods-not-as-described chargeback protections.
  • Written dispute notice must reach the card issuer within 60 calendar days after the charge appeared on the statement, and the issuer has 30 days to acknowledge it — both read directly from the CFPB page linked here. The resolution deadline (within two complete billing cycles, and in no event later than 90 days, after receiving the notice) is set by the statute, 15 U.S.C. § 1666(a)(B), which the CFPB page does not restate; the statute is linked separately above.

Gemological Institute of America (GIA) · Gems & Gemology, Winter 2024

GIA Gems & Gemology — Chemical Analysis in the Gemological Laboratory (EDXRF) ↗

  • Energy-dispersive X-ray fluorescence (EDXRF) provides rapid, non-destructive elemental analysis from Na to U but measures only the shallow near-surface excitation depth.
  • Because XRF is surface-shallow, heavy plating or gold-on-gold plating can skew single-spot surface readings unless paired with mass/density and microscopic inspection.

GIA Lab Notes — Electroformed Gold Piece with Adhered 14K Hallmark Fraud Case ↗

  • Item submitted as '14K solid gold' had a glued-on 14K hallmark disk over a wax/resin core coated with up to ~200 microns of electroformed gold.
  • Standalone surface XRF read 17.99K and 22.47K gold without detecting subsurface core! Fraud was caught by suspiciously low weight-in-hand + binocular microscope examination.
  • Proves why buyers MUST combine calibrated gram scale weight + microscopic hallmark/seam inspection + multi-spot XRF.

U.S. Code (Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School) · 15 U.S.C. § 1666 — Correction of billing errors

15 U.S.C. § 1666 — Correction of billing errors (Fair Credit Billing Act) ↗

  • § 1666(a): on receiving a written billing-error notice within 60 days of the statement, the creditor must send a written acknowledgement within 30 days (unless it resolves the dispute in that time).
  • § 1666(a)(B): the creditor must correct the account or send a written explanation within two complete billing cycles, and in no event later than 90 days, after receiving the notice.
  • § 1666(b)(3): a 'billing error' includes goods or services not accepted by, or not delivered in accordance with the agreement to, the cardholder.
