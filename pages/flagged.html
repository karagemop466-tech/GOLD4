Flagged for Review.

Everything this project could not confirm from an official source, every contradiction found on a retailer's own page, and every policy clause that can cost you money. These items do not block publication — they block a purchase until you check them.

⚠ NO HALLUCINATIONS — read before using any number

No hallucinations. This project manufactures no values. A price, weight, karat, deadline or contact detail is only shown if it was read from the official page linked in the same row. The consumer rules being applied come from the FTC's guidance on buying gold jewelry and 15 U.S.C. § 295.

  • Blank means blank. If a retailer did not publish a gram weight, the weight cell stays empty — it is never estimated from photos, blogs or "typical" rings.
  • Every row carries its source. Open the source link and confirm the number yourself; prices change without notice.
  • Derived ≠ claimed. Gold content and gold value are arithmetic on published values at the captured spot price, and are labelled as derived.
  • Anything uncertain is published on Flagged for Review with the reason and the source, instead of being quietly filled in.

Irregularities found during verification

Blue Nile's own copy contradicts its spec

On the 14K Comfort Fit 2.5 mm page the description says the ring is "crafted in 18k yellow gold" while the specification block says 14K Yellow Gold. On the 14K Riviera Pavé page the description says the diamonds are "set in 14k white gold" while the title says 14K Yellow Gold.

Recorded on BN-04 and BN-08. Ask the seller which metal the ring you receive will be.

A "14K" ring with platinum prongs

The Classic Four Prong Solitaire description mentions platinum prongs while the title and specification list only 14K Yellow Gold. If the prongs are platinum, the gold weight is lower than the ring weight, so the gold value calculation would overstate the gold content.

Recorded on BN-07.

Weights labelled "approximate"

Allurez publishes "Approximate Metal Weight", Anjolee publishes "Approx. Metal Weight", and VRAI publishes a metal weight for ring size 6. None is a measurement of the ring you will receive; a size change moves the weight. Every derived gold value inherits that uncertainty.

No seller publishes a hallmark

Zero of 21 rows show the physical stamp or the maker's mark, which is the one item on the checklist that can only be verified in your hand. The FTC guidance is blunt: if the karat mark has no trademark beside it, don't buy it.

A price that includes the diamond (AN-01)

Anjolee's $870.76 is the price of a configured ring with a 0.50 ct lab-created centre diamond, and its page publishes an approximate metal weight of 2.90 g. The gold content and gold value of the metal are shown, but no price ÷ gold multiple is published for this row: dividing a diamond-inclusive price by a metal-only value would produce a number that looks comparable and is not.

Recorded on AN-01. Compare like with like: setting-only prices against setting-only prices.

VRAI band widths removed on re-verification

The first capture recorded numeric band widths for VR-01 and VR-02. Re-reading the live configuration pages on 2026-10-22 showed only "Band width: Standard" and a 1.6 mm band depth — no numeric band width. Both cells are now blank and flagged WIDTH_UNVERIFIED.

A value that cannot be re-read from the official page does not stay in the dataset.

What would change my mind

A gram weight per size

If a retailer published the weight for each ring size, most of the blank cells here would compute. It is the single highest-value disclosure missing from this market.

An XRF report per item

An assay or XRF result on the actual article would replace the "legal tolerance" range with a measurement. Nothing on a product page can do that.
