Online Buying Checklist

The project owner's buying checklist, verbatim in substance, with pointers to where this site covers each item.

  • Gold purity: Confirm the karat — 10K, 14K, 18K, etc. Higher karat = more gold. (Every row in our tables records the karat as stated by the seller; see Gold Basics.)
  • Solid gold: Listing must explicitly say solid gold, not plated, vermeil, gold-filled, gold overlay, or gold-tone. (All tracked listings state 14K/18K gold metal; see Sellers coverage table.)
  • Hallmark: Look for a 10K/14K/18K stamp plus a maker's/manufacturer's mark. (Not published per item online → left blank + flagged, verify on arrival.)
  • Weight: Check the ring's weight in grams. A legitimate seller should be able to provide it. (Not published on any fetched page → blank + flagged; ask the seller.)
  • Seller reputation: Research independent reviews and complaints; verify the business has real contact information. (Contacts verified from official pages; review-site links provided for manual review on Sellers — scores deliberately not quoted without fetching.)
  • Price: Compare the price with the ring's gold weight and current gold value. An unusually cheap price is a red flag. (Use the Calculator once the seller gives you the gram weight.)
  • Return policy: Make sure you can return it after receiving and inspecting it. Check specifically for exclusions on resized/custom rings. (Blue Nile "Free Returns" verified on product pages; BE policy link pending → flagged.)
  • Payment: Prefer a credit card for purchase protection. Avoid irreversible payment methods.
  • Documentation: Keep the listing, receipt, description, and seller's claims about gold purity. (This site archives listing claims with source URLs + UTC timestamps.)
  • Upon arrival: Before wearing/resizing it, take it to an independent jeweler and ask them to verify the gold purity and weight.
  • Best verification: Ask for XRF testing or another professional gold-purity test; don't rely solely on the stamp.
  • If the seller won't let you independently verify/return it: Don't buy it.
The key: A 14K or 18K stamp by itself does not prove the ring is actually that purity. The safest approach is a reputable seller + proper markings + reasonable price + independent testing after delivery.
