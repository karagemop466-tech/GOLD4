Method

How every number on this site is produced, and what would make it wrong.

The dataset is small because the rule is strict: no value without an official page. This page is the rulebook the audit script enforces.

⚠ NO HALLUCINATIONS — read before using any number

No hallucinations. This project manufactures no values. A price, weight, karat, deadline or contact detail is only shown if it was read from the official page linked in the same row. The consumer rules being applied come from the FTC's guidance on buying gold jewelry and 15 U.S.C. § 295.

  • Blank means blank. If a retailer did not publish a gram weight, the weight cell stays empty — it is never estimated from photos, blogs or "typical" rings.
  • Every row carries its source. Open the source link and confirm the number yourself; prices change without notice.
  • Derived ≠ claimed. Gold content and gold value are arithmetic on published values at the captured spot price, and are labelled as derived.
  • Anything uncertain is published on Flagged for Review with the reason and the source, instead of being quietly filled in.
  • Re-run the check after every review. python3 scripts/audit.py re-derives every number and fails the build on an unsourced value. When you review this dataset, ask of each row: where is the official source, and what date was it read? If a row cannot answer that, treat it as unverified and flag it — including anything added by a previous pass.

The rule

No hallucination

A value is published only if it was read from the official page named in its source_url. If it could not be read, the field stays null and the row is flagged UNVERIFIED. No value is ever estimated, inferred, averaged or copied from a blog, reseller, or marketplace listing.

Only computed from published inputs by scripts/compute.py, using the formulas below. Derived values are labelled as derived and never presented as seller claims.

Assumptions that limit the result

  • Gold weight is taken exactly as the retailer publishes it. Where the retailer publishes 'approximate metal weight', the word approximate is carried into the row notes.
  • No deduction is made for diamonds, settings, solder, rhodium plating or alloy density. The melt value is therefore an upper-bound gold-content estimate of the metal as published, not an appraisal and not a resale offer.
  • Price is the retailer's listed price at capture time and excludes tax, shipping, engraving, appraisal and financing charges unless the row says otherwise.
  • The multiple is a comparison tool only. Retail jewellery prices legitimately include labour, stone-setting, brand, warranty and returns cost.

Legal framework

Solid gold

'Solid gold' means the item is not hollow; it also carries a karat mark showing gold content.

Source: FTC, Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry.

Terms that disqualify an item from this project's shortlist: gold plated, gold plate, gold filled, gold overlay, rolled gold plate, RGP, gold electroplate, vermeil, gold flashed, gold washed, gold tone, bonded.

Hallmark

A karat quality mark must be accompanied by the name or U.S. registered trademark of the company that stands behind the mark. FTC consumer guidance states: if you do not see a trademark with the karat mark, do not buy it.

Sources: FTC guidance · 15 U.S.C. ch. 8.

Project finding: None of the retailer product pages captured for this project published the physical hallmark or maker's mark. Hallmark status is therefore recorded as 'not published' for every row and must be checked on the physical item.

Karat and tolerance

Karat is parts of fine gold out of 24 by weight. 18K = 18/24 = 0.750; 14K = 14/24 = 0.583333; 10K = 10/24 = 0.416667.

For articles other than watchcases and flatware, the actual fineness of the whole article (including solder) may be no more than 0.007 below the fineness stamped on it. So an '18K' stamp legally permits actual fineness as low as 0.743 (74.3%), and a '14K' stamp as low as 0.5763 (57.63%).

Source: 15 U.S.C. § 295.

Payment

Credit cards carry federal billing-error dispute rights; the FTC explains the issuer's obligations and how to dispute a charge. Irreversible payment methods do not carry those rights, which is why the checklist prefers a card.

Source: FTC, Using Credit Cards and Disputing Charges.

What this project deliberately does not do

No reputation score

No independent review data was collected, so no seller is described as reputable or otherwise. The field is empty on purpose.

No estimate of missing weights

"Typical" weights from blogs are excluded. A blank weight stays blank even when a plausible number exists elsewhere.

No price prediction

Spot gold moves. The captured benchmark is frozen with its date, and the site never silently refreshes it.
