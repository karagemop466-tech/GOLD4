Purity

14K, 18K, and what a stamp legally allows.

A karat mark is a claim with a statutory tolerance, not a measurement. Understanding the difference is the whole point of this project.

⚠ NO HALLUCINATIONS — read before using any number

No hallucinations. This project manufactures no values. A price, weight, karat, deadline or contact detail is only shown if it was read from the official page linked in the same row. The consumer rules being applied come from the FTC's guidance on buying gold jewelry and 15 U.S.C. § 295.

  • Blank means blank. If a retailer did not publish a gram weight, the weight cell stays empty — it is never estimated from photos, blogs or "typical" rings.
  • Every row carries its source. Open the source link and confirm the number yourself; prices change without notice.
  • Derived ≠ claimed. Gold content and gold value are arithmetic on published values at the captured spot price, and are labelled as derived.
  • Anything uncertain is published on Flagged for Review with the reason and the source, instead of being quietly filled in.
  • Re-run the check after every review. python3 scripts/audit.py re-derives every number and fails the build on an unsourced value. When you review this dataset, ask of each row: where is the official source, and what date was it read? If a row cannot answer that, treat it as unverified and flag it — including anything added by a previous pass.

The key finding

A 14K or 18K stamp by itself does not prove the ring is that purity. Under 15 U.S.C. § 295 the actual fineness of the whole article may be up to 0.007 below the stamped fineness, so an "18K" ring can legally test as low as 74.3% gold and a "14K" ring as low as 57.63% on this project's karat-divided-by-24 basis. These are conditional statutory illustrations for compliant articles, not measured bounds for a ring with an unverified stamp. Arrange independent professional testing; GIA documents thick plating that XRF alone may miss.

Blue Nile Comfort Fit 2.5 mm — +40.1%

14K is $935 and 18K is $1,310. Without published weights there is no way to prove how much of that gap is gold, which is exactly why the weight column matters.

Brilliant Earth Atelier Solitaire — +16.8%

14K is $1,190 and 18K is $1,390 setting-only, same 2 mm design. Brilliant Earth publishes no gram weight for either row, so again the metal share of the gap is unmeasurable.

Allurez Split Shank — +68.0%

Here both weights are published: 4.40 g in 14K becomes 5.30 g in 18K. Gold content rises +54.9%, so much of the premium is more metal rather than a purer alloy — and the price-to-gold multiple still moves, from 2.57× to 2.79×.

What this means for shopping

Ask for the weight and the karat. A karat upgrade with no weight disclosure is a price increase you cannot audit.
