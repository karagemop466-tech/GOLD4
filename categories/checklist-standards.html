12-Point Gold Ring Checklist, Assay Law & Return Traps

Every practical rule for buying a genuine solid-gold engagement ring online—backed by FTC 16 CFR Part 23, 15 U.S.C. §§ 294–300, NIST Handbook 44, GIA XRF Lab Notes, FCBA credit-card law, and 6 audited seller return policies.

Core Principle: How You Actually Protect Yourself Online

A stamp alone ("14K", "18K", "585", "750") does NOT prove a ring is real solid gold—stamps can be faked in seconds. Real buyer protection requires five layers working together: (1) Reputable accountable seller + (2) Proper karat & registered trademark markings (15 U.S.C. § 297(b)) + (3) Reasonable price vs. raw gold melt value + (4) Independent non-destructive XRF & gram-scale testing immediately after delivery (GIA) + (5) Verified returnability BEFORE resizing or engraving.

Complete 12-Point Online Solid-Gold Ring Buying Checklist

1. Check the exact karat

Look for 10K (41.67%), 14K (58.33%), 18K (75.00%), or 24K (99.9%+). Avoid vague descriptions like 'gold tone' or 'gold jewelry.' Under 16 CFR § 23.3(b), no article below 10 karat may be called 'gold' or 'karat gold' in the U.S.

Official Source: eCFR 16 CFR § 23.3 →

2. Make sure it says 'solid gold' (non-hollow)

Look for 'solid 14K gold' or 'solid 18K gold.' Avoid 'gold-plated,' 'gold-filled,' 'rolled gold plate,' 'gold overlay,' 'vermeil' (gold over sterling silver), and 'hollow' or 'electroformed' gold. Under 16 CFR § 23.3(c), calling a hollow item 'solid' is deceptive (see Mejuri Bold Chain Ring IRR-CAT-10).

Official Source: FTC Jewelry Guide →

3. Look for hallmark details + registered maker's mark

Real gold rings are usually stamped inside the band with 10K/417, 14K/585, 18K/750, or 24K/999. CRITICAL: Under the National Gold and Silver Stamping Act (15 U.S.C. § 297(b)), any quality stamp MUST be accompanied by the manufacturer's or seller's registered trademark or legal name—and a stamp alone can be forged.

Official Source: 15 U.S.C. §§ 295, 297(b) →

4. Check the gram weight of the ring

A reputable seller should disclose or confirm in writing how many grams the ring weighs. Extremely light bands (< 1.5 g, like Ross-Simons 0.7 g 18K band IRR-CAT-08) bend easily or indicate hollow construction. On stone-set rings, subtract 0.200 g per metric carat (NIST Handbook 44) to isolate net gold mass.

Official Source: NIST Handbook 44 →

5. Compare the price to raw gold melt value

Compute pure gold grams = net_metal_g × (karat / 24), then multiply by live 24K spot ($129.45/g on 2026-10-22). Retail rings cost above melt due to labor, stones, and margin—but if a 'solid gold' ring is priced near or below raw gold melt, treat it as an immediate counterfeit/plating warning.

Official Source: BullionVault Spot (2026-10-22) →

6. Buy from a seller with real accountability

Verify clear return policy, physical business address, customer support phone/email, written metal & stone specifications, and independent reputation—avoid anonymous marketplace listings.

Official Source: Example Disclosed Seller Policy →

7. Check the return window & hidden return traps

Confirm you can return the ring unmodified after independent testing. Watch for traps we verified on 2026-10-22: Blue Nile's 30-day clock starts at SHIPMENT (not delivery); Menē allows only 7 DAYS and deducts a 10% restocking fee for cash refunds; Catbird allows 20 days ($9 fee) and makes special orders & engraved rings FINAL SALE.

Official Source: Blue Nile Returns Policy →

8. Ask for written confirmation if any spec is missing

Because most major engagement-ring sites omit gram weight or show copy-paste conflicts (e.g., Blue Nile 501670y '18K' vs 'platinum' IRR-CAT-04), email support before paying to confirm in writing: solid non-hollow karat gold, exact karat of BOTH shank and prongs, total gram weight for your ring size, and inner-shank stamps.

Official Source: Mixed-Karat Prong Example →

9. Pay with a method that has strong buyer protection

Pay by credit card rather than wire transfer, Zelle, crypto, or debit. Under the Fair Credit Billing Act (15 U.S.C. § 1666), credit cards provide statutory billing-error dispute rights (60-day written notice window) plus network chargeback rights if an item is materially not as described.

Official Source: FTC Credit Card / FCBA Guide →

10. Verify independently with XRF + scale BEFORE wearing or resizing

CRITICAL SEQUENCE: Brilliant Earth, Blue Nile, and Ross-Simons ALL void return eligibility once a ring is resized or modified! Immediately upon delivery—before wearing or resizing—have an independent jeweler weigh the ring to 0.01 g and run non-destructive XRF fluorescence on BOTH shank and prongs. Per GIA Lab Notes, pair XRF (~10–40 μm surface depth) with gram-weight/density checks to rule out heavy gold-on-gold plating or resin-filled electroforming.

Official Source: GIA Lab Notes (XRF Limits) →

11. Be extra careful with vintage, estate, or marketplace rings

Unverified estate/peer-to-peer listings carry higher risks of worn-out prongs, low-karat repair solder (note 15 U.S.C. § 295 3 ppt vs 7 ppt solder rules), hollow links, swapped center stones, or fake 14K/18K punches.

Official Source: GIA Electroformed Gold Alert →

12. Don't judge by color, shine, or stamp alone

Yellow polish, white rhodium plating (standard on 14K/18K white gold), and inner-shank '14K'/'750' stamps can all look identical on plated brass or silver vermeil. Only seller accountability + written specs + reasonable melt math + post-delivery independent testing + returnability protect you.

Official Source: FTC Consumer Guidance →
